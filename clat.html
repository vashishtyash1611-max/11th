LAW ENTRANCE 01

CLAT — Pattern & Syllabus

The Common Law Admission Test (CLAT) is the national-level entrance for UG and PG law programmes at 22 National Law Universities — among the best 5-year integrated law schools (BA-LLB, BBA-LLB, BCom-LLB) after Class 12. Conducted by the Consortium of National Law Universities, it tests comprehension and reasoning aptitude rather than prior legal knowledge.

Offline · Paper BasedEnglish Medium−0.25 Negative Marking
Enquire for CLAT

Pattern of Exam

ParticularsDetails
Questions120 MCQs (Multiple Choice Questions)
Time2 hours
Marking1 mark per correct answer · −0.25 for each wrong answer
ModeOffline (paper-based exam)
MediumEnglish
Sections5 — English Language, Current Affairs incl. GK, Legal Reasoning, Logical Reasoning, Quantitative Techniques

Section Weightage (Indicative)

Subject / SectionWeightage
English Language22–26 questions · roughly 20% of the paper
Current Affairs & General Knowledge28–32 questions · roughly 25% of the paper
Legal Reasoning22–26 questions · roughly 20% of the paper
Quantitative Techniques10–14 questions · roughly 10% of the paper

Logical Reasoning completes the five sections. Exact section-wise distribution follows the official Consortium notification each year — confirm the current pattern during counselling.

Syllabus

English Language

Passages of about 450 words from fiction, non-fiction, science, business and social sciences — readable by a Class 12 student in 5–7 minutes. Questions test understanding of main ideas, claims and opinions; drawing inferences and conclusions; summarising; comparing arguments; and the meaning of words and phrases in context.

Current Affairs & General Knowledge

Passages of about 450 words from news and journalistic sources. Questions test awareness of contemporary events from India and the world, arts and culture, international affairs and historically significant events — no prior legal knowledge required beyond the passage.

Legal Reasoning

Passages of around 450 words on factual legal situations, public-policy questions or moral-philosophical enquiries. No prior knowledge of law is needed — awareness of contemporary legal and moral issues helps apply the given rules and principles to fact situations and track how rule changes alter outcomes.

Logical Reasoning

Short passages of about 300 words testing argument structure — premises and conclusions; critical analysis of reasoning; deriving conclusions from evidence; inferences, analogies, contradictions and equivalence; and judging argument effectiveness and validity.

Quantitative Techniques

Short data sets — facts, graphs, pictorial or diagrammatic numerical information — followed by questions applying Class 10 mathematics (ratios, basic algebra, mensuration). Read widely, follow the news, and drill speed with accuracy using Class 10 maths material.

CLAT Eligibility

No upper age limit for the UG programme. Minimum 45% in 10+2 (or equivalent) for Unreserved / OBC / SAP categories and 40% for SC / ST candidates.

LAW ENTRANCE 02

AILET — Pattern & Syllabus

The All India Law Entrance Test (AILET) is the gateway to National Law University, Delhi — established in 2008 and counted among the country’s premier law universities. It is the most important law exam after CLAT and is considered a notch tougher, rewarding focused extra effort.

Offline · Paper BasedEnglish Medium−0.25 Negative Marking
Enquire for AILET

Pattern of Exam

ParticularsDetails
Questions150 MCQs (Multiple Choice Questions)
Time120 minutes
Marking1 mark per correct answer · −0.25 for each wrong answer
ModeOffline (paper-based exam)
MediumEnglish
Sections3 — English Language, Current Affairs & General Knowledge, Logical Reasoning

Section Weightage

Subject / SectionQuestions
English Language50
Current Affairs & General Knowledge30
Logical Reasoning70

Syllabus

English Language

Reading comprehension, grammar, foreign words and phrases, para-jumbles, vocabulary, spellings, inference-based questions, synonyms, antonyms, word usage and fill in the blanks. Moderate difficulty — a high-scoring section.

Current Affairs & General Knowledge

Static GK (History, Geography, Civics) plus current GK of roughly the past year — international events, UN bodies, sports, geopolitics, environmental agreements, eminent personalities, awards and honours.

Logical Reasoning

Puzzles, directions, seating arrangements, blood relations, syllogisms, series, analogy and critical reasoning. Fairly simple for a well-prepared student. Legal principles may appear to test logical aptitude, but no legal knowledge is required.

AILET Eligibility

All non–Foreign National applicants must appear for AILET — selection is solely on that basis. Candidates need 10+2 with a minimum of 50%; those appearing for 10+2 in the exam year may apply provided they meet eligibility at admission.

SLAT — Pattern & Syllabus

The Symbiosis Law Admission Test (SLAT) is the entrance for 5-year integrated law at Symbiosis Law Schools in Pune, Noida and Hyderabad. Relatively easier than CLAT and AILET — a well-prepared CLAT aspirant finds SLAT very manageable.

Reading

Comprehension passages testing understanding, inference and vocabulary in context.

General Knowledge

Static GK and current affairs — events, personalities, awards and everyday legal awareness.

Legal Reasoning

Principles applied to fact situations — no prior legal knowledge required.

Logical Reasoning

Arguments, inferences, analogies and critical reasoning sets.

Analytical Reasoning / Quantitative Aptitude

Data interpretation, series, puzzles and Class 10-level numerical ability.

SLAT Eligibility

Passed XII (10+2) from a recognized board with minimum 45% (40% for SC / ST). Students awaiting Class 12 results may apply, subject to submitting the passing certificate at final admission.

LAW ENTRANCE 04

LSAT—India — Pattern & Syllabus

The LSAT—India tests high-level reading, informal reasoning and deductive reasoning — the critical-thinking skills law college and legal practice demand. Developed specifically for law colleges in India and administered online across the country.

Online Across IndiaScaled Score 420–480No Negative Marking

Subject / SectionQuestions
Analytical Reasoning12
Logical Reasoning (1)12
Logical Reasoning (2)12

Syllabus & Scoring

Analytical Reasoning

Structuring relationships and drawing conclusions from sets of conditions and logical puzzles.

Logical Reasoning

Evaluating and completing short arguments across two dedicated sections.

Reading Comprehension

Deep reading of complex passages with questions on structure, purpose and inference.

Scoring

Credit for correct answers with no penalty for wrong ones — attempt everything. Reported as a scaled score between 420 and 480 with a score band and percentile rank.

LSAT—India Eligibility

LAW ENTRANCE 05

NLAT — Pattern, Syllabus & More

NMIMS (deemed-to-be university) conducts the National Law Aptitude Test (NLAT) for its 5-year integrated UG law programmes at the Kirit P. Mehta School of Law — structured like NLU programmes. Admission is offered on NLAT and / or CLAT scores.

Online · Computer Based150 MarksNo Negative Marking
Enquire for NLAT

Exam Pattern

ParticularsDetails
SectionsVerbal Reasoning · GK · Quantitative Reasoning · Logical Reasoning · Legal Reasoning
Questions150 (30 per section)
Total marks150 (30 per section)
Negative markingNo negative marking
Total time120 minutes
Question patternComputer-based objective-type MCQs
AdmissionBased on NLAT and / or CLAT scores

Syllabus

Verbal Reasoning

One-word substitution, jumbled sentences, synonyms and antonyms, sentence and phrase improvement, paragraph completion, idioms and phrases, sentence correction, active and passive voice, direct and indirect speech.

General Knowledge

Politics, event dates, India overview, history, current affairs, books and authors, geography, sports and games, famous personalities.

Quantitative Reasoning

Graphs, mensuration, statistical estimation, basic algebra, numerical information, ratio and proportion.

Legal Reasoning

Torts, criminal law, general legal principles, contracts, international law, intellectual property law, family law, constitutional law.

Eligibility

Qualifying Marks

  • Class 12 or equivalent from a recognized board in any stream with minimum 50% aggregate
  • IB candidates — certificate / DP with minimum 24 credits, passing 3 HL and 3 SL subjects mandatorily
  • Percentile marksheets converted to real aggregate across appeared subjects

Who Cannot / Can Apply

  • Candidates failing any marksheet subject cannot apply
  • Students appearing for final Class 12 exams may apply, meeting conditions within the given timeframe

Application Process

How to Register

  • Visit the official NMIMS website and create an account via Apply Now
  • Pick the test category (NMIMS-NPAT / NMIMS-CET / NMIMS-LAT) with campus preferences
  • Select multiple categories, schools, programmes and attempts as needed
  • Pay the fee, then schedule test dates and retakes — first come, first served
  • Register only once per category — duplicates can block admission

Documents Required

  • Two passport-size photographs
  • Signature scan on white paper with black ink
  • Photograph — 20–50 KB, 200 × 230 px, JPG / JPEG
  • Signature — 10–20 KB, 140 × 60 px, JPG / JPEG
